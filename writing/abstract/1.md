Open-vocabulary instance segmentation leverages pretrained vision-language models to recognize categories beyond the training vocabulary, providing a promising paradigm for understanding diverse underwater objects. However, underwater absorption, scattering, low contrast, camouflage, and complex backgrounds can weaken or mix discriminative local cues, making natural-image pretrained representations insufficient for capturing fine-grained underwater objects, while object-centric evidence already learned during mask generation remains underexploited for open-vocabulary region recognition. In this work, we present a compact single-stage framework that follows a simple principle: enhance weak fine-grained appearance cues while reusing object evidence already learned within the segmentation pipeline. Specifically, we introduce frequency-conditioned selective detail injection, where high-frequency responses provide candidate texture, edge, and local-structure cues, while low-frequency scene context conditions their selective injection into shallow visual representations. We further reuse same-query mask-generation representations as spatially and structurally informed region evidence to complement, rather than replace, frozen CLIP region semantics for open-vocabulary recognition. Our analyses show that low-frequency-conditioned detail injection is more effective than ordinary high-pass gating, while combining the task-adapted region branch with the preserved CLIP branch outperforms pooled CLIP semantics alone, supporting the complementary roles of these representations. Open-vocabulary camouflaged object segmentation aims to simultaneously localize visually concealed objects and recognize categories beyond the training vocabulary. Existing methods typically follow a segment-then-classify paradigm, where generic segmentation models first localize candidate objects and vision-language models (VLMs) subsequently recognize the segmented regions. However, such a decoupled pipeline leaves both stages with incomplete evidence: camouflage segmentation lacks high-level semantic guidance for distinguishing objects from visually similar backgrounds, while region-based classification often discards the global context on which pretrained VLMs rely. We observe that these two stages provide naturally complementary cues: VLM semantics can guide the localization of ambiguous camouflaged objects, while segmentation masks can in turn provide object-aware spatial evidence for open-vocabulary recognition. Based on this observation, we introduce COCUS, a cascaded vision-language framework that enables bidirectional evidence transfer between segmentation and recognition. Specifically, VLM-derived representations are first used as semantic prompts to guide SAM toward camouflaged regions. The resulting masks are then fed back to the VLM as soft spatial priors, highlighting target regions while preserving the original image context for category recognition. A shared VLM further maintains semantic consistency across the cascade without introducing separate recognition branches. Extensive experiments on open-vocabulary and conventional camouflaged object segmentation benchmarks demonstrate that COCUS consistently improves both camouflage localization and open-vocabulary recognition, validating the effectiveness of coupling semantic and spatial evidence within a unified cascaded framework.
